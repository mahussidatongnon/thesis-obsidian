# Result
- Context: Models derived from physical laws (deductive) and those from experimental observations, and operational data (inductive) are often used to support operation optimisation and structural integrity management in mechanical engineering,
- Problem: Theses models are often described as either physics-based or data-driven systems, masking the fundamental hybrid nature that emerges during their development, deployment, and evolution. Although hybrid modeling is frequently implemented in practice, it is rarely explicitly characterized, preventing engineers from intentionally designing hybrid Digital Twins.
- Approach:
	- analyzes two mechanical engineering applications:
		- a fluidic loop optimized through predictive operation and 
		- a pressure vessel life-duration estimation system
	- investigate how hybridization appears at different stages of DT engineering: model creation, operational usage, and model updating.
	- identify when and why hybridization occurs during different stages of DT engineering.
- Result: 
	- hybridization systematically emerges regardless of initial modeling choices
- Conclusion:
	- Recognizing Digital Twins is nowadays as intrinsically hybrid systems provides a framework for designing more resilient and lifecycle-oriented DT engineering processes.
	- proposes that hybrid modeling should not be treated as an optional enhancement but as a foundational engineering principle for Digital Twins
		- then characterizing hybrid models becomes a key lever for transferable DT development methodologies.
