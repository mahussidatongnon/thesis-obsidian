  % Context
CETIM is an industrial research organization active in several engineering domains, including mechanical engineering. Within this context, a Digital Twin (DT) of an existing fluidic loop has been developed to support analysis and engineering activities. Like many industrial DTs, this DT combines knowledge-based models (e.g., first-principles or physics-based models) with data-driven models (e.g., machine learning or statistical approaches). The combination of these complementary modeling paradigms is commonly referred to as hybridization.


% Problem
Despite the growing adoption of DTs in industry, detailed descriptions of how such systems are engineered in practice remain limited. In particular, the concrete realization of hybridization strategies is often only implicitly reported, making it difficult to understand engineering decisions, share experience, and support knowledge transfer across projects. 

% Approach
This paper presents a reusable engineering examplar based on the development of an existing fluidic loop digital twin in a mechanical engineering context and documents both the DT and the hybridization decisions made throughout its engineering process. The digital twin is first described using the characterization scheme proposed by Bentley et al. To clarify the observed engineering choices, the case description is further structured using a complementary hybridization perspective organized along two dimensions, employed as an analytical support for presenting and interpreting the experience.


% Result & Conclusion
Through this industrial case, we report the fluidic loop digital twin in a systematic manner using an established characterization scheme offering a concrete specification for the DT engineering community. Complementing this description with a structured hybridization perspective helps make hybridization decisions explicit and traceable within the reported engineering context. Together, these elements provide a practical way to document DTs and provide transferable lessons learned for managing hybridization strategies in other domains. This structured description can support communication among stakeholders and offers a basis for future comparative analyses and further investigations into hybrid digital twin engineering practices.
